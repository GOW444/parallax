## I1-1

**Statement:** On raw scales the per-person variables are strongly right-skewed (skewness: Energy per person 2.41, GDP per person 2.38, Electricity per person 4.83). A straight-line OLS of energy on GDP per person is heteroscedastic (Breusch-Pagan p = 0.001, R² = 0.72), and its 4 largest residuals are small-population economies (all under 6 M people): Iceland (0.4 M people), Trinidad and Tobago (1.5 M people), Bahrain (1.5 M people), Singapore (5.8 M people). Only Iceland is below the 1 M small-state cut-off and only Trinidad and Tobago and Bahrain produced fossil fuels in 2016, so these are small, energy-intensive economies rather than the 'small producer states' the plan expected. A handful of such countries dominate distances, fits and axes on raw scales.

## I1-2

**Statement:** Euclidean K-means on the raw shares and intensities gives k = 8 (the largest k tried; silhouette has no clear peak) with a mean silhouette of 0.36, which is 'weak' structure (Kaufman & Rousseeuw). 7 of 8 clusters are named after one source, and the clusters largely re-label each country's dominant source (ARI = 0.66). The shares are a composition: every row sums to 100 (within ±0.004), the 8 shares have rank 7 (one dimension is redundant) and 64% of their pairwise correlations are negative, partly forced by the fixed sum. The smallest cluster (C8, high Solar share, n = 8: Cook Islands; Honduras; Namibia; Palestine; Samoa; Tonga; Vanuatu; Yemen) is split off by a share with a small spread (sd 4.1 pp, centre 17.3%), although 88% of its grids are mostly Oil: z-scoring inflates small absolute differences in minor sources. Cluster C6 (high Energy per person, n = 10) is defined by scale, not by the mix: it puts Iceland (28 gCO2/kWh) with Brunei (500 gCO2/kWh). PC1 (23% of variance) is driven by Oil share, Carbon intensity, Hydro share. Raw shares in Euclidean space therefore group countries by their largest source and by scale, not by how their mix is changing.

## I1-3

**Statement:** Summaries of the low-carbon share of electricity look flat. Between 2000-02 and 2016-18 the median country changed by +0.6 pp (middle half -2.6 to +8.0 pp), and the global share of the balanced panel (35.8% in 2000, lowest 32.0% in 2007, 38.2% in 2021) has no significant monotonic trend (Mann-Kendall tau = 0.14, p = 0.367; Hamed-Rao p = 0.576; Sen's slope +0.09 pp/yr). These summaries hide large moves in opposite directions: 69 of 201 countries (34%) moved by more than 10 pp, 42 up (largest: Sierra Leone 11.2% -> 74.5%, Denmark 16.1% -> 66.3%, Falkland Islands 0.0% -> 50.0%, Cambodia 7.0% -> 53.3%) and 27 down (largest: Congo 100.0% -> 41.2%, Rwanda 100.0% -> 45.6%, Tanzania 95.5% -> 43.0%, Ghana 82.4% -> 45.1%). The ranking is broadly kept (Spearman rho = 0.91), yet the median country moved 15 places. The plan's 'large change' therefore holds for 34% of countries, not for the typical one: a 2018 snapshot cannot tell a country that rose to its level from one that fell to it or stayed there.

## I1-4

**Statement:** The iteration 1 Random Forest barely beats a mean predictor: cross-validated R² = 0.02 ± 0.10 (baseline -0.01), MAE = 167 vs 177 gCO2/kWh for the baseline. Its errors are systematic by the dominant source, which it is not allowed to see: median residual (actual - predicted, gCO2/kWh) Hydro -205, Nuclear -159, Gas +59, Oil +151, Coal +185 (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.001, significant): hydro- and nuclear-dominated grids are predicted too dirty and gas-, oil- and coal-dominated grids too clean. The errors do not concentrate by income or size: median absolute error by 2018 GDP quartile Q2 126, Q3 133, Q4 (richest) 149, Q1 (poorest) 184 (p = 0.379, not significant), and small state 123, other 146 (p = 0.633, not significant). Largest errors: Lesotho (Hydro, -536), Hong Kong (Coal, +494), Botswana (Coal, +466), Lebanon (Oil, +446), Puerto Rico (Oil, +429). Only Population, Gas production per person (2016) and Coal production per person (2016) have a mean rise in held-out MAE, when shuffled, larger than its sd, and the largest rise (12 gCO2/kWh) is small next to the MAE; GDP per person and Oil production per person (2016) add nothing on average. Single-year income, energy-use and producer features carry little information about how a grid is built; iteration 2 tests whether history and context add any.
